About the Kigali Genocide Memorial

The Kigali Genocide Memorial is a powerful and moving tribute to the more than one million victims of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda. Opened in 2004 on the 10th anniversary of the genocide, the memorial serves as a place of remembrance, education, and healing for survivors and visitors from around the world.

Located in the Gisozi district of Kigali, the memorial is the final resting place for more than 250,000 victims. Through its exhibitions, gardens, and education center, the memorial tells the story of the genocide, honors the victims, and works to prevent such atrocities from happening again.

Please Note: The Kigali Genocide Memorial deals with sensitive and emotionally challenging content. Visitors should be prepared for graphic images and stories. The memorial should be visited with respect and dignity.

Historical Context

Between April and July 1994, Rwanda experienced one of the darkest chapters in human history. In just 100 days, approximately one million Tutsi and moderate Hutu were systematically killed in a genocide that shocked the world. The international community largely failed to intervene, and Rwanda was left to rebuild from unimaginable tragedy.

The Kigali Genocide Memorial stands as a testament to the resilience of the Rwandan people and their commitment to building a peaceful, unified nation. It serves as both a warning from history and a symbol of hope for the future.

The Memorial Experience

Permanent Exhibitions

  1. The 1994 Genocide Against the Tutsi - A comprehensive exhibition documenting the events leading up to, during, and after the genocide, featuring photographs, video testimonies, and artifacts.
  2. Wasted Lives - A heart-wrenching exhibition about children who were killed during the genocide, including photographs and personal stories that humanize the statistics.
  3. Genocide Around the World - An educational exhibition examining other genocides throughout history, including the Holocaust, Armenia, and Cambodia, emphasizing the importance of prevention.

Memorial Gardens

The beautifully maintained memorial gardens provide a peaceful space for reflection and remembrance. The gardens include:

  • Mass graves where over 250,000 victims are buried
  • The Wall of Names listing identified victims
  • Reflection pools and contemplative spaces
  • Native plants and flowers symbolizing renewal and hope

Visitor Guidelines

✓ Please Do

  • Dress modestly and respectfully
  • Maintain silence in exhibition areas
  • Take time for quiet reflection
  • Ask questions respectfully
  • Use the counseling service if needed

✗ Please Don't

  • Take photographs inside exhibitions
  • Use mobile phones in galleries
  • Eat or drink inside buildings
  • Touch artifacts or displays
  • Make inappropriate comments

Support Available: Professional counselors are available on-site for visitors who may find the content emotionally overwhelming. Don't hesitate to seek support if needed.

Education & Research

Beyond serving as a memorial, the site houses an extensive education center and archive that works to:

  • Document survivor testimonies and preserve historical records
  • Educate Rwandan youth about their history and the importance of unity
  • Train educators, journalists, and community leaders in genocide prevention
  • Provide resources for researchers and scholars studying the genocide
  • Host international exchanges and conferences on peace-building

The memorial works with schools across Rwanda, teaching students about the genocide and promoting messages of peace, reconciliation, and unity.

Voices of Survivors

The memorial preserves thousands of video testimonies from genocide survivors. These powerful first-hand accounts ensure that the victims are never forgotten and their stories continue to educate future generations.

"We must remember so that it never happens again. Not just in Rwanda, but anywhere in the world. The memorial is not just about the past—it's about building a better future." - Survivor testimony excerpt

Visitors can view selected video testimonies in the exhibition halls. These stories are difficult to hear but essential for understanding the human impact of the genocide.

How You Can Help

The Kigali Genocide Memorial is managed by Aegis Trust, a UK-based organization dedicated to genocide prevention. The memorial relies on donations to continue its important work. Ways to support include:

  • Make a Donation: Contributions support education programs, memorial maintenance, and survivor assistance
  • Sponsor a Student: Help bring Rwandan students to the memorial for educational visits
  • Volunteer: International volunteers assist with education programs and research
  • Spread Awareness: Share what you learn with others to promote genocide prevention

Donation boxes are located throughout the memorial, or you can donate online through the Aegis Trust website.

Location & Getting There

Address: KG 14 Ave, Gisozi, Kigali, Rwanda

District: Gasabo, Gisozi sector

How to get there: The memorial is located about 10-15 minutes from downtown Kigali. It's easily accessible by taxi, moto, or ride-sharing apps. Most taxi drivers know it as "Gisozi Memorial."

Transportation Tip: Many hotels can arrange transportation to the memorial. It's also common to hire a taxi for the day, which allows the driver to wait while you visit.

A Message of Hope

While the Kigali Genocide Memorial confronts visitors with the darkest aspects of humanity, it also celebrates the remarkable resilience and spirit of the Rwandan people. Today, Rwanda is a peaceful, unified nation that has made incredible progress in just three decades.

The memorial's message is not one of despair, but of hope—hope that by remembering the past, learning from it, and working together, we can prevent such atrocities from ever happening again, anywhere in the world.

"Remember. Unite. Renew." - Memorial motto
